Route candidates: which pass did Hannibal cross?

Which of these seven passes Hannibal crossed in 218 BC is a standing scholarly debate (conventions §2), attributed here from the project fact base. "Backers" are the modern scholars whose readings are recorded in the fact base; each argument is marked inferred, reasoned from the attested ancient anchors named alongside it. Source file: data/content/02/route-candidates.md.

R1 — Col de la Traversette
Backers: Mahaney (soil-core studies); MacDonald; Ball. Argument (inferred, per Mahaney et al.): peat-bog cores at ~3000 m carry biomarkers, horse tapeworm eggs, and clostridia DNA dated ~218 BC, indicating a mass animal crossing. Ancient anchors: Polybius on the treeless snowbound summit, the leukopetron, and the 1.5-stadia landslide.
R2 — Col de Clapier
Backers: Connolly; Lazenby; Lancel; Kuhle and Kuhle (contra Mahaney); Hoyte 1960 expedition. Argument (inferred): direct northern route matching Polybius's march along the river past the Island, 800 stadia; feasibility shown by Hoyte's elephant crossing, 1960. Ancient anchors: Polybius 3.49–56.
R3 — Col du Montgenèvre
Backers: Lancel (followed by Miles). Argument (inferred): a known easy trade pass; the one route matching Livy's itinerary up the Durance. Ancient anchors: Livy 21.31–32 (Tricastini, Tricorii, Vocontii, the Druentia crossing).
R4 — Little St Bernard
Backers: Mommsen; Capes; ancient backer Coelius Antipater (crossing "by the heights of Cremo"). Argument (inferred): literal reading of Polybius's along-the-river march as far as Vienne, over Mont du Chat, descending via the Salassi to the Insubres. Ancient anchors: Polybius (arrival among the Insubres); Coelius Antipater fragment.
R5 — Great St Bernard (Alpis Poenina)
Ancient etymological tradition only (named for the Poeni). Refuted by Livy 21.38: the Poeninus deity, hostile tribes, and a wrong arrival among the Libui. No modern backer in the fact base.
R6 — Col de la Larche
Backer: Lancel, as a southern alternative bypassing Scipio's coastal scouts. Ancient anchors: the same Livy Durance corridor.
R7 — Mont Cenis / Petit Mont-Cenis
Named in the fact base as a classic candidate; no backer or distinct argument recorded. Displayed as such.
